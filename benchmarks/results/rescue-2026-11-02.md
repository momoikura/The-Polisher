# Muse Wonder as a rescue, 2026-11-02

**It didn't help, so it's off in the app.** Muse Spark does all the work in the loop.

The idea: when CodePolish's revisions keep being rejected and the fix isn't proven, one revision goes to Muse Wonder and through the same gate, at most twice per run. We tried it on the 4 issues left unproven on 2026-11-01 that a run could still fix ([list](../rescue-2026-11-02.json), [results](rescue-2026-11-02.jsonl)). pyparsing#693 was left out because of the file size limit.

| Issue | Rescues | What they did | Proven | Cost | 2026-11-01 cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sqlglot#8752 | 2 | First rejected for a regression, after costing $0.37. Second cut off by the time limit. | no | $0.4007 | $0.0434 |
| astroid#3255 | 1 | Cut off by the time limit before it returned. | no | $0.0189 | $0.0204 |
| rich#4238 | 0 | | yes | $0.0223 | $0.0307 |
| pygments#3247 | 0 | | yes | $0.0270 | $0.0193 |

No revision by Muse Wonder was kept. rich#4238 and pygments#3247 were proven by Muse Spark alone, and neither was proven on 2026-11-01. rich#4238's whole-suite run didn't finish, so it only has its related tests (24 of 25), and it stays not clean.

Two things follow:

- **Rescues come too late.** They start 11 to 15 minutes into a run, close to where polishing stops at 15 minutes, and a Muse Wonder revision can take minutes.
- **Single runs vary a lot.** The same issues, on nearly the same build, went from unproven to proven with no help. The 11 of 16 in [final-2026-11-01.md](final-2026-11-01.md) is one sample, and could land a little higher or lower on another day.

Four issues is a first look, not a measure. Earlier rescues might do better, but we had no evidence for that and didn't build it.
